ADA Title II deadlines

Two compliance dates, one standard.

The Department of Justice issued the Title II web rule in 2024. An interim final rule published on April 20, 2026 moved each compliance date back one year. Population counts come from the 2020 Census, and an agency inside a larger government uses that government’s population.

April 26, 2027Compliance date for public entities with a population of 50,000 or more
April 26, 2028Compliance date for entities under 50,000 and special district governments
WCAG 2.1 AATechnical standard for web content and mobile applications
1 yearExtension the April 2026 interim final rule granted to each date

The added year gives an institution room to assess first, then remediate against a documented plan.

What the rule covers

Every public channel an institution runs.

  • Web pages and portalsPublic pages, resident portals, and online forms meet WCAG 2.1 AA.
  • Mobile applicationsApps a public entity provides or makes available meet the same standard.
  • Electronic documents in active usePDFs, agenda packets, planning documents, and bids that residents use to reach a service.
  • Vendor-published contentThe rule covers web content a public entity provides directly or through a contract, license, or other arrangement.
Five narrow exceptions

Archived web content, preexisting documents not used to reach a service, content posted by unaffiliated third parties, individualized password-protected documents, and social media posts made before the compliance date fall outside the standard. The institution still provides an accessible format on request.

WCAG 2.1 AA leaves other ADA obligations in place, including effective communication and reasonable modifications. The duty to maintain accessible content continues after the compliance date.

Sources: Federal Register, April 20, 2026 and ADA.gov Title II web rule.

The assessment toolkit

A toolkit that runs from first scan to monthly report.

01
Compliance scan.

Automated review of each public document against WCAG 2.1 AA, with a severity rating and a criterion reference for every finding.

02
Risk report.

A graded summary of findings that ranks public exposure and sets the remediation order.

03
Remediation.

Tagging, reading order, alternative text, and table structure corrected in priority order.

04
Ongoing monitoring.

01 Why accessibility is structural

Accessible services are planned, then built.

Title II of the Americans with Disabilities Act requires state and local government web content and documents to meet WCAG 2.1 Level AA. That includes the website, the forms, and every PDF published on a public page.

When accessibility is added at the end, each new document and page repeats the same defects. When it is part of the content structure, the staff workflow, and the vendor requirements, the service stays accessible as it grows.

Language, content structure, documentation, governance, and implementation requirements belong in the same plan.

03 ADA and WCAG architecture and remediation

From the page template to the document archive.

  • Page and template architectureHeadings, landmarks, navigation, forms, color contrast, and keyboard access defined at the template level so every new page inherits them.
  • Document and PDF requirementsTagging, reading order, alternative text, and table structure for agenda packets, planning documents, procurement bids, and public forms.
  • Remediation planningAn inventory of existing content, a priority order based on public use and exposure, and a sequence the institution can staff and fund.
04 Governance, documentation and accountability

Accessibility the institution can maintain and prove.

Governance frameworks

Roles, review points, and standards that decide who publishes what, and how it is checked before it goes live.

Staff workflows

Authoring and publishing steps that produce accessible content by default, with training materials staff can keep using.

Quality assurance documentation

Testing records that show which content was reviewed, against which criteria, and what was corrected.

Public accountability records

Accessibility statements, feedback channels, and a documented record of remediation progress.

05 Procurement and implementation

Requirements that carry into every contract.

Vendor and implementation requirements translate the accessibility plan into solicitation language, acceptance criteria, and delivery checkpoints, so each vendor builds to the same standard.
