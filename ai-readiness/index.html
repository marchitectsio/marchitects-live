AI Readiness Assessment

AI performs as well as the structure of the institution it serves.

Marchitects assesses the content structure and governance behind a public institution’s digital services. The assessment produces a scored baseline and a sequenced roadmap for AI adoption.

01 Why readiness comes first

AI tools publish on behalf of the institution.

A resident chatbot, an AI-drafted notice, and an automated meeting summary each speak in the institution’s voice. Title II of the Americans with Disabilities Act covers web content a public entity provides directly or through a vendor, so an AI tool that publishes on the institution’s site falls inside that scope.

Records obligations and plain language standards apply to the same output. The institution answers for every sentence an AI tool publishes in its name.

An institution that structures its content and governance before adoption keeps control of what its AI tools say.

02 Six dimensions

What the assessment measures.

  • Public informationThe clarity, plain language standard, and ownership of the content residents rely on.
  • Content and document structureTemplate quality, PDF tagging, metadata, and how readable each archive is to residents and to software.
  • AccessibilityWCAG 2.1 AA conformance of pages, forms, documents, and every AI interface a resident will use.
  • Records and dataRetention rules, the source of truth for each public record, and the audit trail behind published content.
  • Governance and disclosureDecision rights, staff workflows, and the public statement that tells residents where AI operates.
  • Procurement and vendorsContract language for accessibility, data handling, and records, applied to every AI vendor.
03 What the institution receives

Four documents the institution can act on and show.

Readiness scorecard

A scored baseline across the six dimensions, with the evidence behind each score.

Sequenced roadmap

Priority actions ordered by public exposure and available staffing, so leadership knows what happens first.

Policy and disclosure language

A draft AI use policy and a public disclosure statement, written in plain language for council and staff review.

Procurement requirements

Solicitation and contract language that binds each AI vendor to one accessibility and records standard.

04 Four readiness stages

The assessment places the institution on a clear scale.

STAGE 1
Unstructured.

Content, documents, and ownership vary by department. AI use grows through individual staff choices.

STAGE 2
Documented.

Standards exist on paper. Templates, inventories, and vendor lists record what the institution publishes.

STAGE 3
Governed.

Review points, decision rights, and procurement language operate across departments.

STAGE 4
Operating.

Staff publish accessible content by default. The institution reports its AI use and its accessibility record to the public.

05 Connection to ADA Title II

The accessibility dates set the pace.

Every AI interface a public entity offers must meet WCAG 2.1 AA once the entity’s compliance date arrives. The readiness assessment and the accessibility assessment share one inventory, so the institution reviews each service once.

Common questions

Before you schedule.

Who is the assessment for?+

Local governments, special districts, public agencies, and nonprofit service providers that carry public contracts. Any institution that publishes content residents rely on fits the scope.

Does an organization outside government use the same assessment?+

Yes. Healthcare providers, associations, regulated businesses, and universities run the same six dimensions. The procurement dimension covers customer contracts for those organizations, and the disclosure dimension covers customer notices.

What does the assessment review first?+

Public content and published documents come first. Internal records and vendor contracts follow in a scope the institution approves.

How does the assessment connect to ADA Title II?+

Title II requires state and local government web content and mobile applications to meet WCAG 2.1 AA. The accessibility dimension of the assessment measures that standard, and the roadmap sequences remediation against the institution’s compliance date.

What happens after the assessment?+

The institution owns the scorecard, the roadmap, and the draft language. Marchitects builds the roadmap through the Assess, Architect, Build, and Commission process on the Compliance page when the institution commissions that work.
